# Afterstreet (formerly Last Block): Game Design

This page says what the game is and why. [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) says what is built, what comes next and which art each step needs; the two share the same area names (see "Design areas and where they stand" in the roadmap). [ART.md](ART.md) is the look, [SHAPED.md](SHAPED.md) the art catalogue, [TESTING.md](TESTING.md) the test steps. Updated 19 October 2026, at version 0.49.0.

## The pitch

Afterstreet is a lightweight, low-poly, first-person survival game played on your real city, starting with Springfield, MO. Aliens and Missouri weather are the threats. The whole game lives on a USB stick or SSD and runs on whatever device it is plugged into.

Every player owns a world on their own drive. Friends visit each other's worlds, and the items and creations they pick up come home with them. The game is built to be modded and passed around drive to drive.

Design rules:

- Simple, appealing visuals: faceted low-poly pieces with flat colours, cozy days and scary nights (see [ART.md](ART.md)).
- Feature rich but small: content is generated by code or stored as small readable files.
- Anyone can play with anyone: better hardware gives a better view, not a different game.

## How it runs

The game is one folder on a drive, opened directly in a web browser. There is no launcher, installer, host app or website; everything runs from the drive.

| Folder | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `play.html` | The start page and the game in one file, so a single file can be copied to any device |
| `world` | This drive's persistent world and its edits |
| `characters` | The player's roster of passports |
| `packs` | Named content packs: items, building redesigns, alien types, scenarios |
| `maps` | City map packs made by the map maker |

- **Solo play** works offline on every device.
- **The lobby page** shows who is connected and whose world is on offer. The group picks which world to enter.
- **Saves stay on the drive.** The game asks permission to use its folder; where a browser does not allow that, saves are exported and imported as files.

| Device | Play solo | Host a world | Join a world |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windows, Mac, Linux computer | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Chromebook | Yes, from the drive | Yes, scaled down | Yes |
| Phone or tablet | Yes, from the drive or a copy on the device | Yes, scaled down | Yes |

The game scales itself to the device. Phones and tablets get touch controls, a shorter view distance, fewer active aliens, and a smaller active area of the city.

A weaker device picks one of two roles when it joins:

- **Low-detail first-person:** the same game with a shorter view distance and fewer effects.
- **Co-pilot:** no body in the world. The co-pilot manages the map, inventory and crafting for one partner player.

## Multiplayer and hosting

One device hosts its own world and the others connect to it directly, browser to browser. There is no server, host app, website or internet requirement.

- **The host runs the world:** aliens, weather, loot and time. Other devices draw the view and send inputs.
- **Connections:** ethernet cable, shared Wi-Fi, or a phone hotspot. Bluetooth works by pairing the devices as a network; it is slow and short-range, so it suits two players.
- **Pairing:** each visitor swaps a one-time code with the host, by scanning a QR code or pasting text, once per session.
- **Getting the game:** every player needs the game folder, copied from a friend's drive. During a session the host decides each time whether to send a visitor their game version and packs.
- **Group size:** designed for 2 to 6 players.
- **If the host drops,** the session ends. Each visitor's character and items were autosaved to their own device.

## Passports and travelling items

Each character is a passport file on its owner's drive, and it is the only thing that moves between worlds. It holds the character's look, background, skills, inventory, and the designs of any custom items carried.

1. **Join:** the visitor's device sends the passport to the host.
2. **Play:** the host tracks what the visitor finds, crafts and loses.
3. **Autosave:** the host sends the updated passport back every few minutes.
4. **Leave:** the final passport is saved to the visitor's drive, and new item designs are added to their game.

Rules:

- **What comes home:** whatever the visitor is carrying, plus anything the host agrees to share on request, such as a building redesign.
- **What an item is:** a shape, stats, and behaviors chosen from a built-in list (explodes, heals, lights up, shoots). Items carry no program code, so they are safe to copy to any device.
- **Designs copy, items don't:** a blueprint can spread freely, but each physical item must be found, traded, or crafted.
- **One-of-a-kind items:** a maker can mark an original as unique. It can be handed over but never copied, and it records its owners and the worlds it has been in.
- **Host's visitor rule:** each world sets one rule for incoming gear and skills: accept everything, cap to this world's level, or approve new items one by one.
- **Dying with a unique item:** it stays where the character fell, in that host's world, until someone picks it up.

## Modding and packs

Content is made in an in-game editor and saved as small readable files, so it can also be edited by hand or with an AI assistant.

- **What can be made:** items, furniture, building looks and interiors, alien types, character parts, backgrounds, loot tables, and scenarios.
- **Map edits in your own world:** restyle and refurnish buildings, add new structures and props, and demolish buildings. The real street layout stays.
- **Named packs:** shared content travels as a pack with a name, author and version. Packs can be switched on, off, or removed cleanly.
- **Clashing edits:** when a shared pack changes a building the player has also changed, the game asks per building: keep mine, take theirs, or save theirs as an alternate.
- **Game versions:** a whole game version records its name, version and which version it was copied from.

## The world

One world covers a whole city, built from OpenStreetMap data and loaded in pieces around the players.

- **Map maker:** a tool on the start page. Type a city name once while online and it builds a map pack; after that the map is offline and can be shared drive to drive.
- **Loading in pieces:** only the area near players is active. Building interiors are generated from each building's real outline on approach and discarded on leaving.
- **Real places keep their meaning:** hospitals and pharmacies hold medicine, police and fire stations hold ammunition, grocery stores and restaurants hold food, warehouses and hardware stores hold materials.
- **Streets and addresses:** buildings keep their OpenStreetMap house number and street, shown on a plate by the door and on the top line of the screen when you are inside. Roads take the street name their addressed neighbours give them, signs stand along named roads, and the big map writes the names along the roads. Cars are common: parked at buildings, and abandoned in the lanes of highways (mostly wrecks and burnt-out shells) so a drive down a motorway is slow and dangerous.
- **Loot refill:** a looted building slowly regains a little loot over in-game days, less each time.

## Survival

The threats are aliens and the weather, and the tone is scary: stealth and shelter matter as much as fighting.

| Alien | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Swarmers | Small, numerous, weak alone; they alert their pack |
| Stalker | Rare hunter that tracks sound and light, worst at night; learns your hiding spots |
| Giant | Huge and rare; feels sprinting and engines from far off |
| Spitter | Keeps its distance and lobs globs you can dodge (from night 4) |
| Nests | Take over buildings; packs come out of them. Destroying nests is the long goal |

How aliens behave (the rules every alien follows):

- **They do not know where you are.** They find you by sight (blocked by walls and doors, shorter at night unless you carry a light), by noise (how you move, gunfire, engines) and by light at windows.
- **Packs roam.** Aliens from a nest travel as a pack: a leader picks the next building to search, favouring places the pack has not been and places that have been noisy, and the pack turns to a noise together.
- **They lose interest.** Chase, search where they lost you, give up and go back to wandering; a horde walks back to its nest at dawn.
- **Horde nights.** Every third night the nests empty and packs sweep the town. Pacing keeps it fair: no new pack while eight or more are already on you.
- **Weather** follows Missouri's seasons, with a body temperature meter alongside health, hunger and thirst. Events: heat waves, ice storms, thunderstorms, rare tornadoes. Counters: clothing, fire, shelter.
- **Death:** the character respawns at their bed or, failing that, the home safe house (in the south-east corner of the Springfield map), with skills intact. What they carried stays where they fell.
- **Layout rules (interiors):** every room is at least 2.6 m across; a room above its size cap is cut again across its long side; every partition has a doorway on floor on both sides, and doors are kept clear of furniture; a walk test from the front door (or the stair landing) removes only what blocks the way to something you can search, and puts back the rest a room at a time; no room over 9 m² is left bare; furniture budget grows with the building.
- **Building rules (player-made):** walls snap to a half-metre grid, to the line of nearby walls and buildings, end to end, at right-angle corners (the new wall runs on half a thickness so there is no notch) and in T joins; barricades snap onto window and door openings; a wall cannot overlap or cut across another wall, stand in water or on ground that falls more than 1.1 m across its length; walls on a slope sink 0.3 m below the lowest ground and follow the highest. The ghost shows green when snapped, amber when free and red when invalid, with posts at the wall ends it can join.
- **Bases:** players claim a real building, barricade it, and add storage, a bed, and crafting stations. Placing walls, fences, beds, chests and benches uses a ghost that snaps to a half-metre grid and to nearby walls.
- **Salvage:** furniture and wrecked cars can be taken apart with an axe, crowbar or wrench for wood, scrap and parts to build with.
- **The map:** a journal map that only shows where you have been, with flags you plant yourself.

## Characters

Each player keeps a roster of characters on their drive and picks one when entering a world.

- **Look:** low-poly parts and colors: body shape, head, hair or hat, outfit pieces.
- **Background:** chosen at creation, for example Medic, Mechanic, Scout or Builder.
- **Skills:** improve by use.
- **Visiting:** how strong a visiting character is follows the host's visitor rule.

## Communication, travel, traders and goals

- **Radios:** in-game chat and map markers work only in range unless a player carries a crafted radio.
- **Co-pilots:** linked to one partner's radio. Without a working radio, the co-pilot can only place map markers.
- **Bicycles:** quiet, common, no fuel.
- **Cars and trucks:** fast and carry cargo, but need fuel and repair, and their noise draws aliens.
- **Traders:** a few fixed trading posts.
- **Other survivors:** non-player survivors to find and recruit at your base, each with needs and a job (guard, gardener, scavenger). Built in 0.49.0: up to three follow you, eat from the supply chest, and take a job at the base (guard, gardener or scavenger). Since 0.49.2 aliens bite survivors that are in reach (health 60, heal inside the safe zone), followers and waiting survivors fight back, followers cross into the next map square with you, and a waiting survivor sits down.
- **Scenarios:** the long-term goal is a pack chosen by the host: reclaim the city, escape, endless survival, or one a friend designed.

## How the design is built

The ten foundation steps below were all built in the first versions of `play.html`. Everything after them (framed buildings, shaped art, jumping, weapons in hand, placement and salvage, spitters, horde packs, map memory) is in the roadmap's version table, and what is next is in its "Next versions" table.

| Step | Built | Simpler than the design, or missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Drive layout and start page | Start page and game in one file, touch controls, saves in the browser and on the drive | Start page and game were merged into `play.html` so one file can be copied to a phone |
| 2. Hosting | Direct device-to-device play, host's map sent to visitors | Pairing codes are copied and pasted; no QR codes. A host cannot send the game itself |
| 3. Passports | Survivor and inventory travel and come home; visitor rule | Rule is accept, cap at 5, or empty-handed; no item-by-item approval |
| 4. Whole-city map | Map maker by place name, compact map files, buildings loaded in pieces | The map file is still read whole into memory, so phones may not manage a whole city |
| 5. Aliens and weather | Swarmers, stalker, giant, spreading nests; seasons, storms, tornadoes, warmth | Seasons last 4 in-game days |
| 6. Character creator | Look, background, three skills, roster | |
| 7. Items and packs | Workshop, behaviors, crafting, packs, one-of-a-kind items | Workshop is on the start page; packs hold items and scenarios only |
| 8. Map editing and bases | Walls, bed, chest, repaint, demolish, sharing changes on request | No new buildings or room layout changes; clashes ask one question for all buildings |
| 9. Radios, co-pilot, vehicles, traders | Chat and markers with radio range, co-pilot map view, bicycles, cars, trading posts | Co-pilot cannot manage a partner's inventory or crafting |
| 10. Scenarios | Endless, Reclaim, Escape; packs can add variants | Pack scenarios can only vary the three built-in types |

## Open questions and risks

- **Prototype performance is unmeasured**, and it has not yet loaded a real OpenStreetMap export.
- **Saving to the drive.** Only some browsers let a page write into a folder on the drive, and each asks permission first.
- **Solo play on phones and tablets.** Opening the game straight from a drive is unproven on phones. Android likely can. iPhone and iPad restrict running files this way; if they refuse, the only ways in are a web page or an app, which this design rules out.
- **Serverless connections.** Direct browser-to-browser play with pairing codes is unproven for this game, especially when opened from a file and over Bluetooth.
- **Whole-city size.** How large the Springfield map pack is, and how long the map maker takes, are unknown.
- **Passport editing.** A passport is a file a player could edit; the host's visitor rule is the only safeguard.
- **Mismatched versions** between a visitor and a host are not yet handled.
- **Leaving with a host's unique item** after a disconnect needs a rule.
- **Phone limits.** Touch controls, and fitting a whole-city world in a phone's storage and memory.
- **Still to design:** crafting recipes, farming, the list of item behaviors, trader goods, noise and stealth rules.

## Multi-storey buildings (0.49.4)

- Storey height 3.0 m; a building has floors = (height - 2.6) / 3 + 1, up to 14 (large ones, over 14 m tall, may be up to 1800 m² and still have upper floors).
- Supporting walls: the first two levels of room splitting are bearing walls, seeded from the footprint and the ground door, so they are identical on every floor; inner walls differ per floor. Columns stand on a 6 m grid fixed to the building in big open floors.
- Stairs: two shafts, A serving flights 0, 2, 4 and B serving flights 1, 3, 5; each floor is generated when first needed. If a floor's way up cannot be reached from its way in, the building stops at that floor (roof hatch there).
- The floor an entity is on is its level number; world height is level x 3 m + local height.

## Wall construction (planned for 0.56)

Walls follow real light-frame building so they look right when broken open:
- Layers through the wall, outside to inside: outer skin (siding, brick, sheet metal), sheathing, the frame, inner skin (plasterboard, panelling). Each layer has its own strength and breaks on its own; the frame is the last thing to go.
- Frame: a bottom plate, studs every 0.5 m, a double top plate; king and jack studs either side of every opening with a header above, and a sill and cripple studs under a window; corners a three-stud post; blocking between studs half way up.
- Floors: joists every 0.5 m on the bearing walls (the same walls on every floor, as in 0.49.4) with a plank subfloor; roofs: rafters or trusses every 0.5 m.
- Materials: wood studs for houses and small buildings, light steel studs for shops and flats, a steel frame (columns on a 6 m grid, beams, deck) for offices and big stores, concrete (columns, core, slabs, block) for the biggest buildings and bunkers.
- Storey height: 3.0 m houses, 3.5 m shops, offices and community buildings, 4.5 m and up industrial, halls and warehouses.
- Harvest: skins give boards, plasterboard scrap, siding or bricks; the frame gives lumber, studs, plates, steel sections and nails. A frame cut out is worth more than the skins.

## Versions and the offline copy (0.56.2)
Every script is requested as `?v=BUILD`, every Springfield square as `?d=DATAV` (hash of all squares and map.js, in assets/dataver.js). The service worker serves these addresses saved-first (they never change), fetches the page itself fresh, and on activation deletes every other game cache and any square from an older map. Run `python3 tools/stamp_release.py <version>` for each release.

## Harvest (0.57.0)
Wall bays are salvage targets in the same system as furniture and cars (findSalvage, salvInfo, doSalvage): tool decides time, material decides yield, noise draws aliens. Taking apart a bay yields about half what building it cost, so a loop of building and dismantling always loses. Nails and sheet metal are substitutes when building walls.

## Guns (0.61.0)
GUNS in play.html holds each gun's ammo type, damage, pellets, spread, rate, magazine, reload time and noise. Guns are inventory items (the pistol is free); ammo kinds are items. The loaded rounds live in P.mag. Noise follows the gun (bows near zero). Held art uses the held pack's gun models.

## Power plant (0.61.1)
plantObj() picks the site; world.edits.plant.s is the stage (0 burnt, 1 needs fuel, 2 ready, 3 running). gridOn() is true before day 3 or once stage 3 is reached. The plant pack (power.js) loads with the walls pack; only the control panel is drawn so far.
